## Verbatim quotes

> Tiny stock is down 48% since the entire business went public in 2023. Margin pressures have crippled earnings, and the durability of their business model is questionable.

> All of the portfolio businesses are given only ~ one month of capital to stay afloat, and all of the profits are pushed back up to the holdco level to be reinvested.

> Tiny implements Munger’s philosophy of ‘management by abdication’, leaving his CEOs to run Tiny’s portfolio companies with very little interference.

> Except for Dribbble (benefits from network effects), almost all of Tiny’s portfolio companies are either services-based, ‘digital widget’ businesses that aren’t technically difficult to replicate or direct-to-consumer companies.

> Only 19% of their revenue comes from recurring sources.

> Tiny still generates most of its revenue from its digital services segment (most of which is Metalab). But services businesses aren’t great businesses. In 2023, this segment had only ~6.7% EBIT margins and was unprofitable on an NPAT basis.

> On a cashflow basis, their debt load was over 27x cashflow from operations – which feels really high.

## Notes

Public-holdco failure mode: asset quality mix, services revenue, leverage, no product-level coherence. Weakness is capital-structure and portfolio quality, not a missing feature.
